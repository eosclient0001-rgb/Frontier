# FRONTIER
### A racing game at the edge of a world that is running out of itself.

**One line:** A dead shared-world game is still running. What's left of its population races through the shards that haven't unloaded yet — and the world only spends geometry on racers somebody is watching.

**Why it feels like Ready Player One without becoming a cameo parade:** the thing that makes that race scene work isn't the brand names. It's that *anything can be on the track, the rules change mid-lap, and the person who wins is the one who read the world correctly.* That's the design here. The collision is genre-on-genre — a marble quarry cut by stone organs, a weather archive with a paper typhoon — not franchise-on-franchise. Nothing licensed shows up to wave at you.

**What it is not:** neon cyberpunk, meme humor, streamer-bait writing, or "it's Mario Kart but with a twist." The palette is dust, rust, brine, resin, manila, diesel. The world is *worked*, not *cool*.

---

## 1. CORE PILLARS

### Pillar 1 — You must be seen to win.
The Frontier is under-resourced. It cannot render everything at full fidelity, so it renders what the crowd is looking at.

- Standings you can see: **Seen** and **Unseen**.
- **Seen:** the world is solid, rich, and lethal. Bridges exist. Hazards are real. Your car has weight and texture.
- **Unseen:** the world thins to wireframe and stops spending on you. You go faster, you pass through walls — and you also fall through floors. Nothing holds you. Brakes are a suggestion.
- Attention is a resource. Rivals can *look away* from you to thin your reality — ignoring someone is an attack. The crowd can dump attention on a racer and watch a bridge load under them mid-air.
- **You cannot win while unseen.** The finish line will not load for an audience of nobody. You have to spend the last straight being watched, which means the win is always a performance.

This is the game's engine and its theme in one rule: *being watched makes you real; being forgotten makes you fast and fragile.*

### Pillar 2 — One body. The road is made of losers.
You get one run per race. No rewind, no respawn.

- A wipeout retires you. Your wreck stays where it happened and becomes part of the track for everyone behind you, permanently, for the season.
- This is why the world looks like it does: **the guardrails are last year's cars, the statues are last year's racers, the debris is last year's shortcuts.** Every scene's set dressing is somebody's failure.
- You rebuild from what gets salvaged. Your rig is a frame from your wreck, skinned in matter harvested from a world (salt, glass, bark, slag, paper), with one instrument bolted on that was made out of a world's rule (a tuning fork from the Quarry, a horn from the convoy, a stamp from the Adjustment).
- Skins change physics **and** hazard interaction: glass handles heat but shatters on impact; bark regrows; salt dissolves in brine; paper is the fastest thing in the game and catches fire.

### Pillar 3 — Every race has a trick.
Each scene has a rule that is not in the manual, discoverable only by reading the world. Most racers never find it. Finding it is the entire competitive ceiling.

This is the "back up at the start line" idea done as a system rather than a one-off gag. Every scene below lists its trick.

### Pillar 4 — Debt.
The world keeps receipts. Every gross cut you take — skipping a gate's intent, breaking the rules of a world, riding a hazard you weren't licensed for — is written down. It gets charged back later in the same race, or in the hands of another racer in a later match.

Debt is visible on the leaderboard. There are two ladders: **time** and **ledger**. The cleanest racer in the world is as famous as the fastest.

## 2. HOW 90 SECONDS PLAYS

Concrete example, mid-pack, third lap of **The Boneyard Sky** (scene 4):

You're on the ground in a scrapyard when three tornado columns shift. The debris field overhead — a racetrack made of floating wrecks — tears open. Half the field lifts. You don't. You're Unseen down here, so the ground has gone thin and grey, which is why you're fast, and also why the road is starting to not be there.

You take the wide line, get watched by a grandstand, and the world solidifies *around* you at speed: a lightning strike fuses a glass road across the sand, and it loads for you because 400 people just looked at you. You ride it. It's still warm. Behind you a racer who spent their attention on a bridge gets the glass half-formed and sinks a wheel into wet sand at 300.

Two racers are already flying. You can see the convoy horn language ahead — the three-tone that means a hauler is about to change the corridor. You know the horn. They don't. You go under the changing wall instead of around it, and it costs you nothing.

At the line the finish won't load until someone watches you cross. In the last two seconds you have to give the crowd a reason. That's the game.

## 3. THE DWELLERS
### Creatures. None of them animals. Several aren't alive at all.

They aren't enemies with health bars. They're *rules that move*. You can't kill any of them. You can only learn what they're for.

| Dweller | Class | What it does to a race |
|---|---|---|
| **The Cantor** | Acoustic / mineral | A stone instrument the size of a cathedral that inhales. Its intake pulls racers into a lower gallery — a live reroute of the track. |
| **The Bellows** | Mechanical / thermal | Furnace lungs. Each breath softens the track, throws everything off the ledges, and is the only updraft strong enough to clear the last ravine. |
| **The Bloom** | Mineral tide | Supersaturated brine. It doesn't kill what it touches. It *plaques* it. Losers become statuary, and then they become the guardrail. |
| **The Head** | Botanical | A single flower that opens once per race and releases a shared hallucination. Every racer sees the same false finish line. |
| **The Adjuster** | Archival | Ribbons of red tape and stamp heads. It won't chase you. It will *amend* you — your class, your engine, your name. |
| **The Foreman** | Industrial | One autonomous hauler that will not stop for anything, carrying a town on its back. It is a moving track segment with an opinion. |
| **The Downpour** | Meteorological | Rain with intent. It falls in columns, like it's walking. Where it stands, the ground opens and the buried world shows through. |
| **Cold Tenant** | Thermal | A cold that lives in one building and moves one room at a time. It never enters the sun. Neither can you, if you're inside it. |
| **The Hush** | Acoustic absence | A zone where sound doesn't work. Engines starve, horns die, and the only way through is to coast on your own momentum and read lips of the terrain. |
| **Clinker** | Molten mineral | A boneless lump of furnace slag that eats traction. It leaves the road glassy behind it. You can follow it to a shortcut or get ahead of it and find the road gone. |
| **The Understudy** | Algorithmic | A racer-shaped absence that mimics your last run. If you slow down, it takes your place on the grid. |
| **Ghost Lines** | Data / dust | Dust silhouettes replaying the final run of dead racers, telemetry-accurate. They drive through hazards and they always hit the same wall at the same time, every lap. Follow one and you learn where the wall is. |

## 4. HAZARD TOOLBOX
### The stuff a track does to you. Each hazard is a mechanic, not a damage number.

**ACID RAIN — the revealer.** Rain etches. It strips topsoil and *reveals* an older, faster surface underneath — but the surface dissolves as it's revealed. Each segment is usable for roughly six seconds. Rain is not an obstacle; rain is a door that is closing. Second use: rain only eats certain skins, so the same shower is lethal to glass and irrelevant to bark.

**TORNADOES — the second track.** A tornado is a moving floor. Whatever it's carrying *is* a road, and it's a road with a shelf life. Columns migrate on a pattern; the pattern is the level design. Eyewall lap: one scene races entirely inside the rotating wall of a single storm.

**SANDSTORM — the abrasive.** Grit strips your skin's properties off over a lap, so the storm is a clock on your build rather than your health. It also charges you: a charged chassis can be discharged once, and the arc is good for exactly one shortcut through a wall.

**EXPLOSIONS — track edits.** Nobody in the Frontier has guns. Explosions are geology and industry: quarry charges, blast doors, methane, cargo. They open routes and they close routes. The best racers bait explosions a half-second early and ride the new line through the smoke before anyone else can see it exists.

**CRACKING ROADS — the visible clock.** The road cracks in a pattern that predicts where it fails. Read the pattern, you know which lane is gone in four seconds. Ignore it and the failure is yours. Late-race, cracking is the *lap timer rendered in the world*.

**TRAFFIC — moving terrain.** The convoy doesn't block the road, the convoy *is* the road: it only exists where the vehicles part. Three-hundred-ton haulers, permanent motion, no mirrors, and one language — horns — that tells you what the corridor is going to do twenty seconds before it does.

## 5. THE SCENES

Each scene is a full race location. Anatomy: **World → First 10 seconds → Dweller → Track & dynamics → The trick → What kills you → What losers leave behind.**

### SCENE 1 — THE QUARRY CHOIR
**World.** A working marble quarry in a country that no longer exists. The mountain isn't being cut. It's being *played* — kilometers of stone organ pipes are bored through the galleries, and the whole valley hums.

**First 10 seconds.** White dust hanging in dead air. A bass note you feel in the chassis before you hear it. Your mirrors shake off their mounts.

**Dweller.** The Cantor: an instrument the size of a cathedral that breathes in. Its intake pulls rock, dust, and racers into a lower gallery. Falling in isn't death, it's a reroute — you come out somewhere else on the track with a different car than you had.

**Track & dynamics.** Sound waves travel through the air as *ridable crests*. Hit one at the right speed and angle and it carries you — you are airborne on a wavefront, no wheels touching anything, for as long as the note lasts. Wrong speed and the standing-wave field shreds you. Blast faces shear off on a timer (cracking roads). Quarry charges go off on the workers' schedule (explosions). Marble dust holds every racing line ever driven through it — including the lying ones rivals leave on purpose.

**The trick.** A lap is counted as *a full round of the score*, not a loop of the road. Slow to the tempo of the piece and the Cantor carries you around instead of you driving it. Veterans also know the rest bar — one silence mid-piece — where the intake reverses and skips you straight to the exit gallery.

**What kills you.** Standing waves at the wrong speed. Falling in during a *fortissimo*. Being on a blast face when the note ends.

**What losers leave behind.** A hollow on the wall in the shape of a car, still humming.

---

### SCENE 2 — THE ADJUSTMENT
**World.** This is where weather is filed. Aisles of weather files kilometers tall — storms stacked as paper, held in manila, indexed by decade. The track runs the stacks. Rain that never fell is in here, waiting to be reassigned.

**First 10 seconds.** Total silence. Then the sound of a single stamp, far away, and a wall of files breathing out a smell like old rain.

**Dweller.** The Adjuster: ribbons of red tape and stamp heads, moving through the aisles. It does not pursue. It *amends*. If it reaches you it corrects you — your class, your engine, your name — and you race the rest of the lap as whatever it decided you are.

**Track & dynamics.** **Red tape** strips across the road. Drive through it and you're bound: drag, tangled steering, a lap that gets slower while you fight it. Cut it and you're free and fined (Debt). **The Redactor** sweeps the stacks periodically and erases sections of track with a black bar. Whatever fills the gap is pulled from the world's memory of what a previous racer *described* — so the road ahead is substituted with geometry from other, wrong worlds, wearing the wrong physics.

**The trick.** Erasure is a hole, and holes are writable. Racers who broadcast a description of what should be in the gap — and get the crowd behind it — effectively *write the road ahead of themselves*. The fastest lap in this scene is authored, not driven.

**What kills you.** Being described badly. Substituted geometry that looks like road and isn't. A paper typhoon that strips the paint off you and the years off the world.

**What losers leave behind.** A red bar across a stack, and one more sheet in it.

---

### SCENE 3 — SALT TIDE
**World.** A salt basin under a moon that pulls a tide of saturated brine instead of water. Once a day (once a race) a red wall of dissolving water comes across the flats from the wrong horizon. The sea is behind you. It stays behind you by about ninety.

**First 10 seconds.** Flat white to the horizon. Then the horizon turns red, and the red has a *sound.*

**Dweller.** The Bloom is the tide itself. Anything it touches doesn't die — it plaques. It plasters a racer in crystal and installs them where they stopped. The road's guardrails are last season's losers. The corner markers are a friend of yours.

**Track & dynamics.** Crystal arches grow across the track pre-tide and halve your usable line every thirty seconds. Salt crust is grip right up until it's glass. Some of the "guardrails" are still, barely, drivers.

**The trick.** Don't outrun the tide. Race the tide *front* — the leading edge is a liquid slope, and if your wheels are hot enough you can surf it, gaining on everyone who thinks it's a wall. Cold wheels and the front takes you.

**What kills you.** Stopping. Cold. The moment you decide you've got enough lead.

**What losers leave behind.** Statuary in racing posture, facing forward, eyes open. Good landmarks. Everyone uses them.

---

### SCENE 4 — THE BONEYARD SKY
**World.** A scrapyard that harvests tornadoes. The wrecks have been lifted into a permanent aerial debris field, and the debris field is a second racetrack. The race is two levels and you're meant to switch, not choose.

**First 10 seconds.** Ochre dust, a wall of stacked cars going up past the top of your screen, and three tornado columns standing still on the horizon like a decision.

**Dweller.** The Foreman: one autonomous hauler the size of a district, carrying a town on its back, crossing the yard on no schedule. It's a moving track segment that will let you ride it — and it turns.

**Track & dynamics.** **Fulgurite roads**: lightning strikes the sand and fuses a glass road for about four seconds. The route ahead is being written by lightning. You're following a storm that lays the road a half-second in front of you, and misreading it means glass that isn't finished. The aerial field only holds what the columns are currently carrying; when the columns move, the sky opens and the cars come down. Sandstorm grit strips your skin's properties over a lap (abrasion clock). Explosions here are cargo, not weapons — a contested pallet detonating in the columns dumps a hundred wrecks across both levels at once.

**The trick.** The farm's real product isn't power, it's *height*. Fastest lap goes up early and stays up, riding the debris field while it exists, and treats the ground as a mistake. The second half of the trick is the horn language: three descending tones means the corridor is closing in twenty seconds.

**What kills you.** Glass that's still wet. Cars that are still falling. The Foreman's turn.

**What losers leave behind.** The field gets denser at the height where people die most. You can read the danger in the layers.

---

### SCENE 5 — THE GLASSWORKS
**World.** A working glassworks on a scale that shouldn't be possible. The track is being blown *as you drive it* — drawn out of a furnace ahead of you, cooling behind you, existing in roughly a forty-second window.

**First 10 seconds.** Orange light on grey brick, and a road that isn't finished yet, and men in leather aprons who don't look at you because they're working.

**Dweller.** The Bellows: the furnace lungs. Each breath softens everything at once, throws unbolted things off the ledges, and briefly turns the road elastic. Traction becomes a timing puzzle.

**Track & dynamics.** **Cracking roads as the clock**: cooled glass propagates fractures in a pattern, and the pattern *is* your lap timer. Molten drips fall like slow rain. The annealing section narrows into a tunnel that seals behind you. Nothing here is trying to kill you — the whole scene is just *a process*, running, and you are inside the process.

**The trick.** The Bellows' exhale is the only updraft in the world strong enough to clear the last ravine. Everyone else hides from it behind the kiln walls. The lap record belongs to someone who went and stood in it.

**What kills you.** Standing still on hot glass. Being under a drip. Being in the annealing tunnel when it closes, which is quiet and takes a long time.

**What losers leave behind.** Glass, with you in it, on the wall of the completed tunnel, permanently — the glassworks has a wall of finished pieces and everyone knows what it is.

---

### SCENE 6 — ROOTWORKS
**World.** A single plant the size of a county is expanding through an abandoned city, thirty meters a minute. The race is held inside the growth zone. Streets are being eaten and replaced by the plant's structure as you drive.

**First 10 seconds.** Jade light through translucent new growth. A road that used to be a street, now bark. Sounds are wet. Somewhere far off, something enormous is opening.

**Dweller.** The Head — the plant's single flower, which opens exactly once per race. When it opens, its pollen produces a shared hallucination: every racer, all at once, sees the same *false* finish line, in the same wrong place, with the same crowd noise. The lie is unanimous. It lasts about eleven seconds.

**Track & dynamics.** The road fights you by growing toward the racing line. New-growth bark is a ridable living surface — ridges, contraction, sap. Nothing is static: the track is *winning* if you stop. Acid rain appears here as the revealer: it strips old pavement and uncovers pre-city infrastructure underneath, six usable seconds per segment.

**The trick.** The real finish is downwind of the flower. So is the lie. The lie is *thicker* downwind — which means the winning move is to steer straight into the hallucination and drive through the vision while everyone else swerves around it, convinced.

**What kills you.** Trusting a crowd. Being slow enough for growth to close the street behind you.

**What losers leave behind.** Compost, and a street that grows over them in a shape you can still make out from above.

---

### SCENE 7 — THE LONG HAUL
**World.** A continent-spanning autonomous freight corridor. The road only exists where the convoy parts. Hundreds of three-hundred-ton driverless haulers in permanent motion — you race inside the gaps, like racing inside a wall that never stops moving.

**First 10 seconds.** Concrete, diesel haze, and a horizon made of moving steel. The sound is not loud, it's *total*.

**Dweller.** The Foreman (see scene 4) runs this corridor too, and it's the only vehicle that acknowledges racers at all. Everything else will simply not brake.

**Track & dynamics.** Traffic as terrain: under, between, or *on*. Impacts deform the corridor — a jackknifed hauler becomes a new wall and a new route for everyone behind you, for the rest of the season. Explosions are cargo events. The sand-and-diesel mixture in the wheel wells acts like weak sandstorm grit: your skin's properties slowly buff off.

**The trick.** Haulers don't have turn signals. They have horns, and the horn language describes the convoy's own manoeuvres twenty seconds out — it was designed for maintenance crews who drive inside the corridor. Nobody uses it correctly except three people on the leaderboard.

**What kills you.** Assuming the gap is a gap. Assuming a stop exists.

**What losers leave behind.** A deformed corridor, and a wreck that becomes permanent architecture. The corridor is the most human-shaped place in the game.

---

### SCENE 8 — THE DEAD HOURS
**World.** Open highland in acid season. The rain here is a standing weather system with intent — it falls in columns and the columns move like something walking, slow and deliberate, and it etches off the topsoil wherever it stands.

**First 10 seconds.** Pewter sky, rust-red ground, no horizon line, and columns of rain standing still and then stepping, one at a time, toward you.

**Dweller.** The Downpour. Not a storm — a *patrol*. It isn't hunting. It's making its rounds, and it does not care what is in the way, and it will step over your car with a column and the road under you will be gone into a hole fifty meters deep.

**Track & dynamics.** Rain is the revealer: each column uncovers the buried original track — a straight-line route from before the world was patched — and then eats it, six seconds per segment, stone to mud to pit. Two route switches per lap minimum. Cracking appears as *etching*: the acid draws the failure pattern on the surface before it takes the ground away.

**The trick.** The buried route is straight because it's older than the world's geometry. There is exactly one place where it leaves the race boundary — a hole in the definition of the level — and if you follow it you don't cut the corner, you arrive at the finish from *underneath*, which the game counts, because the game has no rule about that.

**What kills you.** Chasing the reveal too deep. Standing in a column.

**What losers leave behind.** A hole with a car-shaped bottom. They become wells. The highland is full of wells.

## 6. MODES

- **One Run.** Solo, one attempt, leaderboard. Restarting is allowed, but your ledger keeps every attempt. The ladders reward first tries.
- **Grid.** Eight racers, one race, one body each.
- **The Long Race.** Multi-day attrition race across six scenes. Cars are repaired between segments with what's salvaged. Most people lose their chassis, not the race.
- **Crew (spectator play).** You don't drive. You spend attention: load a bridge, thin a rival, protect a friend's reality, author a description into an erased segment. The crowd in this game has hands, and they are the reason the winner won.

## 7. WHY THIS IS ACTUALLY BUILDABLE

The core mechanic is **level-of-detail**, which every engine already implements — this design just promotes it from a technical compromise to the rule of the world. Dirty scenes (degraded geometry, missing shaders, wireframe distance) are a *feature*, not a bug budget: art costs go down exactly where fidelity is being withheld on purpose.

Scenes are procedural systems, not bespoke levels: a quarry that plays itself, a tide that moves at 90, a storm that writes glass. Small art teams can ship big-feeling worlds because the world is mostly *rules* and the rules are cheap and the set dressing is generated from the wreckage of other races.

---

## 8. WHAT WE ARE NOT BUILDING

- **No neon cyberpunk city.** The Frontier's built environments are working, dirty, analog, and old. Light comes from furnaces, weather, and moonlight. If a scene needs a color, it gets one: rust, brine red, jade, manila, ochre, glass orange.
- **No cameo dump.** No licensed cars, no "remember this?" tracks. Genres collide; brands don't get invited.
- **No meme writing.** No brainrot humor, no streamer-bait banshee announcer, no ironic tone. The Frontier takes itself seriously and that's what makes it funny and sad in the right places.
- **No power-up roulette.** No blue shell, no random boxes. Everything that happens to you came from the world's rules, another racer's choice, or the crowd's attention. Losses are always legible.
- **No animal creatures.** Nothing that breathes in a normal way. Stone, weather, plants, paper, steel, absence.

## 9. APPENDIX — 22 MORE ONE-LINE SCENES

1. **Mirror Run** — a flooded valley where the track loads only in the reflection. You drive by watching the water instead of the road.
2. **Tenement Ascent** — vertical race up the outside of a mile-tall housing block; the service elevators are moving road segments.
3. **Glacial Retreat** — downhill race on a calving glacier, where the finish line moves uphill as you approach it.
4. **Prayer Wheel** — a mountain of rotating wheels, each one a rotating track segment. Direction is a choice you make four times a lap.
5. **The Understudy** — a mirror race against a copy of your own last run. If you fall behind it, it takes your name.
6. **Paper Typhoon** — the weather archive has a leak. A typhoon made of every storm that was never used.
7. **The Ledger** — a race against your own Debt, which has taken a physical form and is faster when you cut corners.
8. **Irrigation** — a maze of sluice gates that a rival crew is operating against you in real time.
9. **Two Weathers** — a race on the moving front line between two storms fighting over the same valley.
10. **Night Crossing** — a salt pan that's only crossable while its surface is below the dew point. You are racing temperature.
11. **The Long Shadow** — a dusk race where the shadow of a mountain is the lap timer and the sun is the enemy.
12. **Derrick Field** — an oil field of walking pumpjacks with the endurance of animals and none of the minds. Ride the beams; the beams are rhythm.
13. **The Funicular** — a cliffside cable-car city; gravity is the rival and the cables are the road.
14. **Compost** — a city being digested. The road is a process, not a place.
15. **Chalk Lines** — a chalk quarry where the route is drawn by a draughtsman on a gantry. Stay on the drawn line or fall through the drawing.
16. **The Hush** — a soundless valley. Coast or stall, and the only way to know where anyone is, is the dust.
17. **Ledger Bell** — a mine where a bell tolls each shift and the mine reconfigures. Learn the shift pattern or learn the tunnels too late.
18. **Ferry** — a river crossing made of river traffic: you're driving across the roofs of boats that are themselves driving.
19. **Cutwater** — a flooded canyon where the water level rises 2 cm a second. The last straight is underwater and the water is the finish.
20. **The Kiln** — a brickworks race inside a firing kiln. The tunnel you're in is being bricked shut behind you by machines that don't care.
21. **Cold Tenant** — a winter city where a cold that lives in one building has moved into the next one, and the race runs through both.
22. **Chorus Line** — an orchestra of quarry organs, but underground: ride the bass through a stone cathedral's throat.

---

*The game's thesis in one sentence: in a world that only renders what it's watching, the way to exist is to matter to someone — and the road is made of everyone who didn't.*
