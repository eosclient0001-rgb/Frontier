# Next iteration — vehicle and VFX

This is a plan, not an implementation of the changes below. Keep the browser prototype separate from any later C++ port.

1. **Remove the visual tyre carcass deformation.** Delete the `TyreCarcassSolver.js` deformation path and `Shader/TyreCarcass.wgsl` tyre vertex offsets only after replacing the callers in `SurfaceRaster.wgsl`, `ForwardRaster.wgsl`, shadow/card passes, and `IlluminationHost.js`; remove the deformation inspector slider and default. Preserve tyre contact, pressure, thermal/failure, rim radius and wheel dynamics. Check the same static tyre topology in mirrors, Garage and main view; run vehicle stability and headless harness tests.
2. **Sparks: colour, collision and light.** In `SparkSequence.js`, `SparkRaster.wgsl`, `CourseStructure.js` and the static obstacle queries, collide sparks with course *and* world geometry (walls, ramps, props; moving bodies if the cost is acceptable) with contact normals, restitution and lifetime limits. Calibrate blackbody emission from a physically plausible hot-white/yellow core at birth to dim orange/red as Kelvin falls; no abrupt yellow-to-orange switch or overexposed flat yellow streak. Add a small capped light budget for clusters of hot sparks into direct lighting and surface-card refresh, not one light per particle. Verify shadows/occlusion, proximity lighting and no light persisting after a spark dies, including at night.
3. **Derez / rez light.** Connect the existing `EffectSequence.js` emission and `EffectRaster.wgsl` colours to temporary local illumination of nearby surfaces during both release and assembly, with a bounded light budget and distance-field/card invalidation. Test the three presets in day and night scenes without stale afterglow or large perf spikes.
4. **Rim marks after shredding.** `TyreMarkDepot.js` currently stamps rubber from sliding power without checking `Vehicle.Wheels[Index].Shredded`. Stop rubber deposition for shredded wheels. Add a separate narrow, grey metallic scrape/rim-mark channel or material, driven by rim contact/pressure and slide, with no tread grooves; keep tyre marks for intact tyres only. Verify the transition frame on road, kerb and debris, and ensure reset clears appropriate state without erasing older marks.
5. **Steering self-centering and countersteer assist.** The requested term is likely *self-aligning torque* / steering return-to-centre, with optional countersteer assistance. Identify which behaviour is wanted before choosing a strength curve. Add speed- and tyre-grip-dependent centering to the driver-input/steering model (`DriverInputIntegrator.js`, `VehicleSolver.js`), not a hard snap to zero. Offer assist off/on, preserve manual authority, and test straight-line tracking, drift recovery, keyboard/controller release and low-speed parking.

**Physics note:** this WebGPU prototype is **not using Jolt Physics**. `RigidBodyWorld.js` is a custom JavaScript 120 Hz sequential-impulse solver for convex debris/cones and vehicle contacts; `VehicleSolver.js` implements the vehicle dynamics. A future C++ integration can evaluate Jolt, but this plan does not claim that migration has happened.
