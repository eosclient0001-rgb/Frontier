One diagonal tread primitive → double-mirror array

The block from your directional tread is diagonal, so everything follows the diagonal: the end rings are cut perpendicular to the bar's own axis (never horizontal), the array steps along that axis, and the bridge quads land clean. Mirror #1 makes the chevron pair, mirror #2 makes the opposite shoulder. Outline loop → quad fill → array → bridge the gap.

Why diagonal matters

Rings are built from the centreline's own normals, so a diagonal bar gets diagonal end rings. Arrayed along its axis, the next bar's start ring is the same ring, shifted — bridges are clean quads. Cut the ends horizontally instead and every bridge quad becomes a sheared sliver: that's the "very wrong bridges" failure.
