Emitter

Volume-budgeted rain and sediment-carrying runoff. The live terrain uses coarse exchange; Hydraulic shaping uses the refined XYZ field.

Emitter intensity scales this rate. This is a rainfall-volume budget, not a brush-strength multiplier. Bare rock should not disappear during a short storm.

Rain → runoff & settling

Landing starts a separate captured runoff travel budget. Slow runoff evaporation and turbulent suspension keep water and fines travelling downhill. Moving parcels that hit a numerical time limit are accounted as unresolved/outflow, not dumped into a false sediment pocket.

Explicit, uncalibrated material assumptions. Lower resistance describes weak substrate; it is not a measured rock classification.

Routing head helps water cross tiny erosion pits, not deep closed basins. It is a bounded routing approximation—not a full pressure-based fluid simulation.

Settling lasts at most one simulation second and cannot erode. Unplaced sediment and dissolved load remain in the retired/outflow ledger; water is not converted into rock. Continuous rain emits new drops—pause to stop time, or set Emission rate to zero and keep running to drain the pool.

Hydraulic material & rate

Capacity-limited rain and runoff on a weathered surface. Preview scale accelerates represented material exchange, not particle lifetimes. It is not a calibrated geological clock.

1× is the unaccelerated closure; higher values represent more parcels. Particle count controls sampling density, not a direct cutting multiplier. The safety ceiling is applied after all overlapping requests are summed. Hardness maps to an assumed bed-cohesion threshold—not measured rock strength.

          The scene uses WebGL2. Particle motion, erosion, deposition, and
          sculpting run on the GPU using WebGL2 fragment passes. A worker
          handles initial generation and explicit connected-fragment selection.
          WebGPU is not initialized. A floating-point render target is required
          for GPU erosion.
