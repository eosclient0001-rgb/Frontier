UNDER THE SURFACE

Spray in the air. Foam on the surface.

This is a separate dark-themed experiment. The original pond version and its source files are unchanged.

New foam algorithm: Foam density is advected and decayed on a 128-column Eulerian field at 20 Hz. It is shaded in the water material with a pre-baked, mipmapped porous microtexture. Dense regions merge into matte patches and thin regions open into broken edges. There are no surface foam particles or foam draw calls. Only 128–512 airborne droplets remain particles.

Rendering profiles: Balanced refreshes smaller reflection/refraction buffers at most every third frame and at most 20 Hz while stationary. Camera changes refresh immediately. Fast uses environment reflections and an approximate bed in a single scene pass. Full restores every-frame scene optics. The FX CPU metric excludes the wave solver and GPU time.

Stability: effects advance on the same fixed simulation clock, but never modify the wave solver. A fixed field size, a small spray cap, and bounded source strengths limit work. The field receives no feedback from its own shading. The Splash tool applies one controlled primary water impulse and a separate particle burst.

Research: Thürey et al., real-time breaking waves; real-time bubbles and foam; SideFX whitewater lifecycle. The revised texture-based approach follows persistence/decay ideas in Crest and whitecap coverage research. Measurements and limitations are in FOAM-FIELD.md.

Controls: Make a splash, select the Splash tool (5), or move the duck (4). Space pauses the whole simulation. Clear foam & spray removes only the secondary effects. One opening splash demonstrates the effect; there is no continuous emitter unless you enable automatic ripples.

Inspired by the pond in your reference: a horizontal water surface with shaded waves, floating lilies, stone obstacles, and an orbitable 3D view.

The solver integrates a damped wave equation on a fixed-world-space grid, with fixed CFL-limited steps, a second-order velocity-Verlet integrator, symmetric damping, and reflecting boundaries. The duck and automatic disturbances advance on that same clock; leftover frame time is accumulated rather than integrated as a short final step. Water height is a displacement field—not a stretched column of cells.

When maximum slope exceeds a threshold, the entire grid is resampled to a finer level (up to twice your base resolution on each axis, subject to a 160,000-sample budget). Height and vertical velocity are interpolated, and the timestep is reduced. Refinement stays active until reset to avoid repeated resampling. This is global, slope-triggered refinement, not local adaptive meshing.

Water optics: In Balanced/Full modes, all five presets use an index of refraction of 1.333, Snell refraction toward the rendered bed, depth-based Beer–Lambert attenuation, approximate scattering, Schlick Fresnel, GGX sunlight, and planar scene reflections. Ocean wind detail and swamp surface film are visual approximations. Coefficients are representative, not measured water samples. Puddle raises the rendered bed to 6.5 cm below the mean surface; this does not change wave-speed physics.

The rubber duck: Its damped buoyancy follows the surface, slope forces and drag affect its motion, and moving it injects bow/stern waves. It stops at banks and rocks. It is a lightweight coupled toy, not a full rigid-body displacement solve.

It is a surface-wave approximation: no overturning waves, volumetric splashes, full horizontal fluid transport, or engineering-grade accuracy. Lilies use spring-tethered, slope-driven motion; they are not coupled rigid bodies. Large disturbances are bounded for robustness.

Pond size: Set Width (8–20 m) and Length (6–14 m), then Apply dimensions. The basin, bed, render mesh, and solver are rebuilt; the physical cell spacing is preserved rather than stretched. Resizing clears existing waves and resets the floating objects. Automatic ripples and shader wind are off by default; manual interaction still creates waves.

Controls: Orbit to rotate; scroll or pinch to zoom. Ripple to disturb water. Move lily to drag a leaf. Space pauses; R resets; 1–5 select tools. Move duck lets you drag the yellow rubber duck; release it to coast.
